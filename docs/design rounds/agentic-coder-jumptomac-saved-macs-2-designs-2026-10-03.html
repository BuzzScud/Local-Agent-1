What it does now: /jumptomac server-1 jumps and remembers one Mac, the last one. /jumptomac alone goes there, but nothing on screen says so, or which Mac it is.

What both designs add

  • Saved Macs: every Mac you jump to is kept (the last one first), so you pick it instead of typing it.
  • Online or not: read from Tailscale, so you know before you try. An offline Mac says so instead of waiting.
  • Another Mac: type its name once; it is saved as soon as its door answers.
  • Forget a saved Mac when you no longer use it.
  • /jumptomac server-1 typed in full still works as today.
Design 1names as you type: your Macs in the menu under the prompt, the way /jumptomac itself shows there (drawn on the app's grid, colours from the capture)

What changes

  • Type /jumptomac and a space: the menu under the prompt lists your saved Macs, then Another Mac…. Nothing opens; it is the same menu as for commands.
  • ↑↓ pick, enter jumps, tab fills the name in.
  • Typing narrows the list. A name that is not saved yet shows as new: enter tries it and saves it once its door answers.
  • Forget: /jumptomac forget server-1 (the menu lists the saved ones there too).

Design 2a small box: /jumptomac + enter opens "Jump to a Mac", like the app's other pickers (drawn on the app's grid, colours from the capture)

What changes

  • /jumptomac alone opens a box: your saved Macs, each with ● online or ○ offline from Tailscale, then + Add a Mac.
  • It opens on the Mac used last, so /jumptomac enter enter jumps there.
  • + Add a Mac takes its name in the box; enter tries it, and it joins the list once its door answers.
  • ⌫ on a saved Mac forgets it (it asks once). A Mac Tailscale sees but you never used shows under the saved ones as on Tailscale.
