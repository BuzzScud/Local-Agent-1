What was built

Seven parts, all in the terminal half of the repo (terminal/src/agent). They are in the worktree, not in your installed Bonsai. The screen lines are the app’s real output from its end-to-end test.

Helpers: the Finder

Bonsai can hand a look-around job to a helper. The helper starts with its own fresh memory, sees only the job (not your conversation), may only Read, List and Search, and reports back in a few lines. It stays idle until your message is done, so Bonsai can send it a follow-up and it still remembers what it read.

  ⎿  2 reads · 1s (ctrl+o to expand)
  ⎿  found: The CSV header is built by toCsv in export.mjs.
Fable: lead + async subagents [8.13.2] · the screen lines are the app’s own output in its end-to-end test · switch off: BONSAI_HELPERS=off

The Reviewer

After a big change (two or more code files, or 40+ changed lines) a helper reads the change with fresh eyes before Bonsai says done, and ends with “VERDICT: ok” or “fix: …”. A fix sends Bonsai back once. A small change (one file and its test) keeps the quick check, which costs seconds, not a minute.

Fable: each test’s grader, brought inside the run · switch off: BONSAI_HELPERS=off

The rescue when stuck

Repeating a step, 5 errors in a row, out of steps, or its memory filling up for the first time (a trim): instead of stopping, a helper with fresh memory gets the request, what Bonsai worked out and where it looked, and reports the way forward. Bonsai carries on with 15 more steps. Once a message. It thinks at your effort (it was High until you asked for everything at Low); the first-trim trigger came from the first new-code try, which trimmed 3 times in 25 minutes and never met the old triggers.

Fable: a backup model takes the turn [6.4.9] · switch off: BONSAI_HELPERS=off

Several looks per step

Up to 4 Read, List or Search calls in one reply run together; a change or a command still runs alone. Each result goes back in the order the model asked for it.

Fable: Claude Code, the harness several of the card’s tests run in · switch off: BONSAI_MULTI=off

Thinking where it pays (built, off)

One step thinks at Medium on its own when a bug fix starts, after a failed test, after two tool errors in a row, or after a repeated step; at most 4 a message. Built and tested, but off: you asked for everything at Low. BONSAI_ADAPTIVE=on turns it on. Never High: High rewrites the saved prompt from its first line, so every switch would re-read the whole conversation (checked on the real template).

Fable: adaptive thinking [8.1] · switch off: BONSAI_ADAPTIVE=on

Memory beside a helper

Both slots share one pool of 32k. With less than 12k free beside the conversation, the conversation is saved to disk, the helper gets the whole pool, and the conversation comes back after; measured, the next turn then re-reads only 22 tokens. Tried and dropped: the engine’s cache-reuse switch (it did nothing on this model: a 30% middle cut still re-read all 3,750 tokens after it) and reading the High instructions ahead at start (no use once nothing runs at High).

Fable: 1M tokens per agent [8.13.2]

/helpers and esc

/helpers lists every helper of the conversation: its job, time, looks, and how it ended. esc while a helper works stops only the helper; Bonsai carries on (ctrl+c still stops everything).

What was not copied from Fable, and why

A team of five working at onceFable’s team is faster because Anthropic runs five copies of the model on separate hardware. On the M4 there is one model, and two answers at once were measured slower in total (6.4 tokens a second for both together vs 8.05 for one alone, 25 Sep). Five at once would make every agent crawl. Helpers run one at a time instead; their gain is fresh memory, not speed.
A backup modelFable hands a flagged turn to an older Claude. Only one model fits in 16 GB beside the 27B, and a smaller local model would be a worse coder. The rescue uses the same model with fresh memory and High thinking instead.
A safety classifier on every turnFable’s classifiers look for misuse (weapons, cyberattacks). Bonsai’s real risks are commands that damage the Mac, and those are already covered on every call: the blocked list, the question before edits and commands, and the macOS sandbox fence.
1M tokens of memoryThe Mac has room for 32k beside the 27B. Helpers get the same effect a different way: a long look-around happens in a helper’s own memory and only a short report comes back.
Helpers that editYou picked look-only helpers. Editing helpers would still run one at a time, and merging a small model’s edits back is the riskiest part; the fix flow already tries changes in a scratch copy.
