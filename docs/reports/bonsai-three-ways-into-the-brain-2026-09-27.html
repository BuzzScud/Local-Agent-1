What your own conversations say

Your 12 saved conversations: 26 requests, 64 replies, 45 tool calls. This is where the time went, and it is what the tests below were aimed at.

  • You ask for pages and files more than for code fixes: stories in a text file, a finance dashboard, a notes page. All typed in your home folder.
  • A page takes 7 to 10 minutes. notes.html: 603 s for 428 lines. Note.html: 399 s for 141 lines.
  • A quarter of the notes.html time was thrown away. The first write hit the reply limit of 2,048 tokens mid-way and was discarded, about 150 s; the page was then built again in 4 parts.
  • Note.html was written with a stray closing tag. The brain noticed its own mistake, then took 7 steps (2 reads, 5 commands) to make a one-line fix.
  • After every finished page it checks its own work with commands, one to five of them.
  • “Announces a step and stops” happened 0 times in 64 replies. That habit is what I first aimed the push at.

  • It runs on this model after one small fix. The tool as it comes stopped at once: it counts this model’s helper layers as brain layers. With the count set by hand it made the vector in 28 s.
  • Strength 1: every move became “Done”. 10 of 10 at the two moments, in 2 to 19 s each. Without the vector the brain reached for a tool there 156 times of 156, under every setting.
  • It skips a real fix, and it gets careless. At the stray-tag moment “Done” leaves the stray tag in the page. Two of the 10 replies said things it had not checked (“120 lines” for a page of 428, “opened” for a page it never opened), and some sentences lost words.
  • Strength 2, in either direction: the brain answers nothing. 20 replies of 20 were empty. The useful range is narrow.
  • It is all or nothing. The vector is set when the model server starts and then acts on every request, a code fix as much as a page. It has no switch per request, as the push has.

  • It connects. The server loaded both files beside the 27B. Their strength is set by one request while the model stays loaded, in under a second, and twice the strength gave exactly twice the push.
  • Off means off. With both files at 0 every number came back to the digit: 66.2400% before, 66.2400% after.
  • It acts on the brain’s real values. The file built from the real row pushed “ Tokyo” up at all three questions; the one built from the stored row pushed it up twice and down once, by small amounts. The engine’s code says the same: a side file is applied before the storage mix.
  • My hand-made file did less than I designed. After “The capital of France is”, “ Tokyo” rose from place 112 to place 22, not to the top. Either my way of undoing the storage mix is not exact for this matrix, or the brain’s feel for a word is smaller than I assumed. I do not know which.
  • To use this way for real takes training on the full-size model on rented hardware, from many examples of good work, then a test that the result carries over to these 2-bit weights. Your own conversations are 26 requests: far too few to train on. Not tried.

What this says about step 3

  • The sampler is not the lever. Neither the push nor steadier choices changed what the brain does at your real moments.
  • The steering vector is a real lever, with a price. It removes the checking after a page, and with it the chance to catch its own mistake.
  • The pair that would fit your use: the harness checks a written page itself (tags balanced, the file complete) and says so in the result; trusting that result is then safe. The harness half belongs to the other session’s round.
  • The biggest costs in your own use are not choices at all: writing speed, about 13 words a second, and a reply limit that throws away the first attempt at every page.
  • Nothing here is switched on. The two switches in the code are off unless set; the vector and the side files are files in the results folder.

Limits you should know

  • Four moments from two conversations. Your real use, but a small slice of it.
  • 25 moves per setting at the stray-tag moments, 5 at the others. Enough to see a large effect, not a small one.
  • One vector. 12 example pairs of my own writing, one way of making it. Other examples may steer better or worse.
  • No full task run with the vector on. The 28 practice tasks were not run with it, so I do not know what it does to code work. It may skip running the tests.
  • The vector’s slips (made-up details, dropped words) were counted by reading 10 replies, not by a test.
