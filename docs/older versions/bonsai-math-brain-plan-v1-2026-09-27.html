Why a checker is needed

Two reasons. Small models guess at arithmetic, so a tool that computes exactly is the known fix. And the crystalline C code itself gives wrong answers in places — found by the helpers today and on 24 Sep, each reproduced on this Mac. How it works

Two ways in. In a normal conversation the model may call Math whenever it needs a number. A request that is mostly math (or anything after /math) goes to math mode, which makes the calculation compulsory and checks the answer.

1 · sort
Is it math?

Rules first ("is 108733 prime", "factor", "angle of 7 on the 60 ring", "2^200"). Code requests never qualify.

2 · plan
List the sums

The model writes the calculations it needs as Math calls — it does not do them in its head.

3 · compute
Math runs them

Standard answer + their code's answer + a check for each. Instant.

4 · answer
The model explains

Plain words, using only the results it was given.

5 · check
Every number checked

Each number in the answer must come from a result or your question; if not, one retry naming the stray numbers.

The side-by-side box is printed by Bonsai itself, not typed by the model — so the two answers on screen are always exactly what was computed.

> is 108733 prime? ⏺ Math (is prime · 108733) ⎿ Standard not prime — 108,733 = 227 × 479 ✓ 227 × 479 = 108,733 Crystalline prime their test stops at primes under 100 ⏺ No. 108,733 is not prime: it is 227 × 479. When the two answers agree the second line says "same" or "agrees"; when they differ, both stay on screen and the model's sentence names the reason.

The Math tool

One tool, one op word, a few arguments. Whole numbers are exact at any size (JavaScript BigInt). "Theirs" is a faithful copy of their C function — bugs included — proved against their compiled code, so the side-by-side is honest.

Math mode

When it starts

  • Always after /math … (which also attaches your notes, as now).
  • By rules: a question that is mostly numbers and math words — prime, factor, divisible, angle, ring, lattice, base 60, square root, "what is 2^…".
  • Never for requests that name code files or code words (fix, test, function, bug…). A list of 40 ordinary requests must never land in math mode (a test).

What it enforces

  • At least one Math call before an answer.
  • The number check: every number in the answer must appear in a result or in your question (commas, rounding and "about" handled). One retry naming the stray numbers; after that the answer is shown with a warning line.
  • Their notes (on /math) are quoted beside results; where a note and standard math differ, both are shown.

Lean proofs — what's realistic

Lean 4.34 is installed and 37 prime-clock theorems are already proved (~/work/prime-clock-check, 25 Sep). The 27B cannot be trusted to write new proofs from scratch, so the brain uses Lean in two dependable ways.

1 · Proved facts
A searchable list

Every proved theorem with its plain-words statement (the 37 now, more later). "proof" finds the match and answers "Proved in Lean: name", checked with #print axioms (no gaps).

2 · Small proofs
Finite claims, filled in

A claim of the form "for every n below N, …" in whole numbers is written into a fixed Lean template and closed by decide — 60 s cap, run in a copy of the pinned project.

3 · Not promised
Open-ended proofs

"Prove the crystalline thesis" is out of reach for any tool; the brain says which parts are checkable and checks those.

How we'll know it works

Before anything else: measure the 27B alone

A 30-question math bench — arithmetic, big powers, primes, factoring, clock positions, solids, base 60, and five claims from the thesis. Scored today without the brain, then after.

  • Bar: ≥ 27 of 30 right with the brain, 0 unchecked numbers.
  • Every question has an answer checked by two methods.

Tests in the suite

  • Each op against known values (π(106) = 78,498; 2127 − 1 prime; Carmichael numbers not prime…).
  • theirs.mjs against their compiled C (the 24 Sep oracle) — the side-by-side must show what their code really says.
  • The number check catches a planted wrong number.
  • 40 ordinary requests never route to math mode.
  • The box in a real terminal (pty test), light and narrow windows.
